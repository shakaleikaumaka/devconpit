What happened 🎻

Four days in one hall. The declaration became a room.

From Tuesday 3 November to Friday 6 November 2026, the Devcon 8 Music Sanctuary at the Jio World Centre was an open circle: cane stools in a ring, dhols and drums on rugs, guitars and ukuleles on stands within anyone's reach, a mandala of light on the wall. No auditions, no gatekeepers, no wrong instrument. About 35 artists took turns at the mics across the four days — Indian singers, tabla, saxophones, DJs, live coders and first-time players — with India's classical traditions at the heart of the mix.

On the last evening — Friday 6 November, Dhanteras, the first day of Diwali — the Sanctuary became the hall for Devcon 8's closing ceremony: a brass lamp was lit, then confetti fell, and the same room became a dance floor under the same mandala.

The session 🎙️

"In the future music and culture is free" — Alaska · Tomo Saito · Shaka Lei Kaumaka

Friday 6 November 2026, 12:00–12:25 IST, Jasmine Stage 4, track Futures Worth Building. Alaska (DJ, producer, engineer; MDRN), Tomo Saito (designer and art director at the Ethereum Foundation) and Shaka Lei Kaumaka (musician and steward of the Open Source Orchestra) on what Ethereum can do for music once it treats culture as infrastructure instead of "adoption".

Nothing loads from YouTube until you press play (privacy-enhanced youtube-nocookie embed). Recording published by the Ethereum Foundation's Devcon team. Direct link: youtube.com/watch?v=AZ-EUgC9CNM · session page: app.devcon.org/schedule/W8HAES · Shaka's speaker page with the transcript and notes: shakaleikaumaka.com/devconspeaker.

Most music on the internet is technically free, but the systems around it are not. Platforms own the audience, flatten the context, and turn culture into inventory. This session asks what Ethereum can do for music if it stops treating culture as "adoption" and starts treating it as infrastructure for communities that already know how to coordinate.— the session abstract, as published on the Devcon 8 schedule

Fork this pit 🍴

Your festival, residency, camp or conference gets a pit next. Five steps, no permission needed.

# 1 · take the template (code GPL-3.0 · words & art CC BY-SA 4.0)
git clone https://github.com/shakaleikaumaka/devconpit my-pit && cd my-pit

# 2 · make it yours — title, dates, place, the story; your domain in CNAME (or delete CNAME)
$EDITOR index.html CNAME

# 3 · tell the truth in the manifest — your name, your dates, status "declared" until it happens
$EDITOR pit.json          # keep: consent.contact = consent@publicinform.com · takedown link · credits

# 4 · publish — GitHub Pages from the main branch, root folder; point your DNS at it
git add -A && git commit -m "my pit, declared" && git push

# 5 · join the registry — mail your pit.json URL; the next open № is yours
#     contribute@publicinform.com  ·  https://publicinform.com/pits/

Rules that travel with the fork: pointers, not payloads · consent travels · regenerate, don't hand-edit · version honestly · one consent window. The full standard: publicinform.com/spec and PIT Standard V2 (the room, the film, the errata, the road, the live layer, the TV button).
